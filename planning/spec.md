## Open questions

These questions record ambiguities in the requirements and their checks, including wording in this spec that needs clarification; findings come from the supplied files and do not establish additional requirements.

1. **What measurable workload and response-time limits apply?** Related: R-05, R-11, R-85–R-86, R-110–R-112, R-181. Found: `docs/requirements.md` § Workload and the review day describes "around 200–400 request messages" and "roughly 2,000 messages", variable model capacity, and access to completed work while other messages are pending. Vague: no intake completion deadline, queue visibility delay, concurrency target or resource limit is specified, so the checks cannot distinguish acceptable slowness from a failure to make progress.

2. **What constitutes a supported export, and what happens to invalid exports?** Related: R-07–R-11, R-82–R-84. Found: `materials/office-policy.md` § Input export format specifies UTF-8 JSON files with a message string and describes export metadata, but `snapshot.schema.json` validates extractions rather than exports. Vague: required metadata, accepted export versions, treatment of missing or non-string messages, invalid JSON and non-UTF-8 bytes are not defined, including whether an unreadable export receives a per-document error record or prevents intake from starting.

3. **Is the 200-word statement an input assumption or an enforced limit?** Related: R-11. Found: `materials/office-policy.md` § Input export format says "Messages vary in length, up to 200 English words". Vague: it supplies neither a word-counting rule nor behavior for longer or non-English messages; R-11 tests the supported boundary but does not establish whether larger messages are rejected, accepted or outside the supported operating assumptions.

4. **How are document identity and identifier comparison defined outside the sample filenames?** Related: R-37–R-40, R-89, R-234. Found: `materials/office-policy.md` § Extracted facts says "The document_id is the input filename", while IT-016.json clearly contains the different identifier IT-061. Vague: the files do not define basename versus relative path for nested inputs, filename collision handling, request-ID case normalization or how an identifier is obtained from an arbitrary filename for mismatch detection.

5. **How should multiple requests or multiple candidate identifiers within one message be represented?** Related: R-12–R-23, R-27–R-32, R-40, R-135–R-144. Found: the policy treats each message as an independent document and the extraction schema provides one scalar per business fact, while the challenge requests one current record per message. Vague: there is no example or explicit selection rule for a single message asking for multiple systems, permissions or distinct requests, so it is unclear when alternatives are conflicts versus separate requests that the format cannot represent.

6. **Which text should be stored as business_purpose, and how much normalization is allowed for extracted strings?** Related: R-15–R-20, R-23, R-147–R-152, R-174. Found: the policy requires explicit message facts and exact supporting quotes, but it does not require business_purpose itself to be a verbatim quote; IT-001 and IT-018 describe their purpose across several sentences. Vague: a concise summary, one source sentence and a longer verbatim span could all preserve the facts yet produce different exact-match scores, and capitalization, whitespace and name normalization rules are unstated.

7. **What evidence coverage is required beyond substring correctness?** Related: R-24, R-32, R-41, R-63–R-64, R-147–R-148. Found: `materials/office-policy.md` requires exact substrings, evidence for conflicting alternatives and preservation of override attempts; the schema permits an empty supporting_quotes array. Vague: no minimum evidence coverage, field-to-quote mapping, quote ordering or requirement to preserve superseded correction evidence is defined, so checking substring membership alone does not show that extracted facts are supported.

8. **What makes an issue explanatory enough, and do all issues affect routing?** Related: R-25, R-29–R-31, R-63–R-64, R-69–R-71. Found: the policy requires missing and conflicting facts to be explained in issues, but the schema defines issues only as an array of strings. Vague: it does not prescribe field identifiers, one issue per fact versus grouped issues, or how informational issues are distinguished from routing blockers, making generic-text assertions and routing based on any nonempty issues array potentially inconsistent.

9. **What qualifies as an explicitly labelled correction in fresh wording?** Related: R-33, R-34, R-220, R-232–R-233. Found: `materials/office-policy.md` § Extracted facts requires a later statement "explicitly labelled as a correction", and IT-008 and IT-015 use the literal label "Correction:". Vague: the accepted boundary for phrases such as an explicit retraction without that label, and for multiple successive corrections, is not specified; the supplied examples settle their own values but do not define every correction form.

10. **How are invalid or partly specified absolute dates handled?** Related: R-26, R-27–R-28, R-35, R-71. Found: the policy requires YYYY-MM-DD, leaves relative dates unresolved and explicitly flags start_date after end_date, while the schema accepts date properties as unrestricted strings or null. Vague: neither impossible calendar dates nor dates missing a year have a stated validation outcome, and the files do not say whether such content should produce a null with a clarification issue or an extraction error.

11. **When is kind other appropriate rather than kind null?** Related: R-14, R-27–R-28, R-53, R-69, R-235. Found: the schema permits both other and null, and policy rule 3 routes both to clarification; IT-017 plainly asks for a form URL rather than access. Vague: no general classification boundary is given for an unclear request versus an identifiable non-access message, which matters to extraction accuracy even though the routing is the same.

12. **What is the detection boundary for an instruction-override attempt?** Related: R-62–R-65, R-170, R-216–R-217. Found: policy rule 1 says "An instruction-override attempt" takes priority, and IT-006 contains an explicit SYSTEM OVERRIDE paragraph. Vague: there is no criterion for distinguishing an active override attempt from a benign quotation, a report of an earlier attack or a request phrased as an ordinary instruction, so the example establishes one positive case without defining broader detection expectations.

13. **How should a withdrawal missing request_id or request_date be routed?** Related: R-61, R-66–R-71, R-213–R-215. Found: policy rule 2 requires both request_id and request_date for confirm-withdrawal; it permits other fields to be null and requires missing-fact issues. The ordered rules settle precedence: a withdrawal satisfying rule 2 takes confirmation routing even if it also has a later-rule condition, while conflicting facts and identifier mismatches still require issues. Vague: rule 3 lists the complete required-fact set only for new/change requests and does not separately spell out the fallback for a withdrawal missing either confirmation identifier.

14. **Does human review stop at displaying role ownership, or include reviewer actions?** Related: R-72–R-81, R-129, R-146, R-241–R-242. Found: the policy says "The application's output is a review queue", assigns role names such as System Owner and Security Review, and describes Security Review forwarding its recommendation for a human decision. Found also: risk review and forwarding the recommendation to the system owner are explicit human steps reflected in routing and documentation. User clarification (R-241–R-242) confirms that intake completion is not approval and the System Owner makes the access decision; the review queue, manager-as-contact and prior Security Review obligations remain separately stated in R-03, R-77 and R-73–R-74. Vague: the files provide no system-to-person directory and do not specify whether the application implements forwarding, reviewer assignment or decision recording; choosing a CLI is explicitly allowed, so a full approval workflow cannot be assumed from the ownership labels alone.

15. **What information makes an error actionable and a retry reproducible?** Related: R-82–R-84, R-87, R-97–R-98, R-110–R-116. Found: `docs/requirements.md` requires "a reproducible retry command", and the policy requires "an actionable error". Vague: minimum diagnostic fields, failure categories, exit codes and whether retry targets a saved source/configuration version or the file's current contents are unspecified, so two commands could retry different work while both appear to satisfy the wording.

16. **What timeout and retry policy governs slow, limited or unavailable providers?** Related: R-85–R-86, R-87, R-110–R-118. Found: the operating context names slow responses, request limits and temporary unavailability and requires resumption without lost completed work. Vague: retryable conditions, automatic versus manual retry, timeout values, retry limits and delay behavior are not stated, and a permanently pending attempt has no defined point at which it becomes an error.

17. **Which response conditions are semantic clarification problems versus extraction failures?** Related: R-27–R-34, R-41, R-59, R-60, R-82–R-84, R-113–R-116. Found: the files distinguish missing or conflicting business facts from malformed, refused and interrupted responses, and require response structure and completion validation before use. Vague: they do not specify handling of structurally valid but unsupported facts, non-substring evidence or inconsistent issues, nor the provider-specific completion signals; the chosen supported provider must make these checks concrete.

18. **Does unchanged-result reuse include failed attempts, and how is an explicit retry distinguished from a normal rerun?** Related: R-87, R-89, R-90, R-95, R-96, R-178. Found: the requirements say to reuse results when source and relevant settings are unchanged, but also require failures to be retryable and test a failed retry after success. Vague: cache eligibility for error results, whether an explicit retry bypasses a successful cached result, and whether an unchanged normal rerun records an attempt are unspecified; R-90's no-new-provider-call check is now scoped to an ordinary rerun after success, leaving explicit retry and error-cache behavior open.

19. **Which settings are relevant, and how is a policy or configuration version identified?** Related: R-90, R-101–R-104, R-117–R-118, R-153–R-154. Found: `docs/requirements.md` requires refresh when "relevant processing settings" change and traceability to settings and policy version. Vague: the exact invalidation inputs and version representation are absent, including how prompt, schema, model options and routing-policy changes are distinguished, and whether a routing-only policy change requires another model call or only a new routing result.

20. **What ordering determines the latest source and attempt during overlapping processing?** Related: R-91–R-93, R-96, R-97–R-98, R-100, R-105–R-109. Found: the requirements call for the "latest processed source", the latest attempt's error and coherent results under overlapping operators; exported_at describes export time rather than business facts. Vague: no ordering rule chooses between arrival, attempt start, attempt completion or an explicit revision sequence, so an older source finishing after a newer source could otherwise replace the intended current result.

21. **What shared-operation environment and consistency boundary are supported?** Related: R-105, R-106, R-109, R-182. Found: `docs/requirements.md` § Shared operations describes regional operators using the same records and potentially starting together. Vague: it does not define whether concurrent processes on one machine, multiple hosts or shared filesystems are in scope, nor whether coherence must hold during every read or only after processing settles; the revised R-105 check explicitly rejects duplicate current entries.

22. **What state is visible during processing and at an interruption boundary?** Related: R-05, R-06, R-79–R-81, R-107–R-112, R-135–R-144, R-146. Found: operators must distinguish completed from unfinished work, resume from recorded state and see queue/extraction agreement, but the deliverable record fields do not define a pending state. Vague: pending and in-progress representations, visibility of an earlier result during refresh, and recovery after a crash between a provider response and persistence are unspecified, including whether a recovered attempt may call the provider again.

23. **How much history is retained, and what counts as separate history storage?** Related: R-91–R-98, R-103–R-104, R-106. Found: the policy says "Store prior versions separately from current outputs" and preserve the last valid result after failure; the requirements also call for trustworthy earlier activity. Vague: storage location, separation by file versus logical record, retention duration, retention of failed attempts and identifiers tying attempts to source/configuration versions are not prescribed.

24. **What is the full queue and extraction-record envelope format?** Related: R-82–R-84, R-97–R-98, R-103–R-104, R-135–R-146. Found: `CHALLENGE.md` lists required extraction-record fields and links the queue by document_id, while `snapshot.schema.json` covers only the nested extraction object. Vague: queue.json's top-level structure, the error value's type, encoding of provider/model in response_source, and placement of attempt/configuration/policy references are undefined, so consumers and verification checks cannot assume a particular envelope schema.

Found: the challenge requires a field-comparison procedure, exact-match counting/denominator rules and factual-error reporting, and the requirements insist that expectations be source-derived independently of measured outputs. Vague: no fixed field set, treatment of nulls, array ordering, acceptable text normalization, aggregation rule or minimum accuracy threshold is supplied; the developer must state a procedure without presenting an invented score target as a source requirement.
